Hydrostatic Pressure and Backfill Drainage Behind Retaining Walls

Water weighs roughly 62.4 pounds per cubic foot. When rainwater gets trapped inside clay soil directly behind a retaining wall, saturated soil plus water pressure can easily double the outward thrust pushing the wall forward.

Anatomy of a proper drainage zone

  • Clean angular crushed stone column: Typically at least 12 inches wide behind the entire height of segmental block walls (using washed open-graded stone without fine dust so water drops freely).
  • Perforated drain pipe at the base: Placed behind the bottom course with holes oriented cleanly and sloped steadily to daylight out the wall end or an approved storm outlet—covered with a rodent screen so critters do not plug the exit.
  • Non-woven geotextile filter fabric: Separates native backyard clay from the clean drainage stone so fine silt does not migrate in and clog the stone voids over time.
  • Surface swale above the wall: Divert roof downspouts and hillside runoff away from the back of the wall crest.
