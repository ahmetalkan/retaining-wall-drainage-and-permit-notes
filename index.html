Retaining Wall Drainage and Permit Notes: Build Behind the Wall First

A retaining wall is a civil engineering structure holding back tons of earth and rainwater. What shows from the yard—concrete blocks, natural boulders, or poured walls—is only half the build; the buried leveling pad, clean angular drainage gravel, perforated drain pipe, and geogrid tieback fabric hidden behind the wall decide whether it still stands straight five winters later.

The Concrete Masonry & Hardscapes Association (CMHA) publishes design standards for segmental retaining walls, the International Code Council (ICC) publishes model residential building codes establishing height and surcharge thresholds where engineered drawings are required, and the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) documents how water saturation destabilizes hillsides and soil slopes.

Core checklist before accepting a wall bid

Cost guides, repair diagnostics, and height rules appear on Retaining Wall Lab.

  1. Call 811 before trenching: Retaining wall footings sit below finished grade where buried utility lines run.
  2. Confirm base burial depth: Gravity walls require an embedded first course sitting on a compacted crushed-stone leveling pad below the lower finished soil grade.
  3. Require clean drainage aggregate behind the wall: Never allow a contractor to shove excavated backyard clay straight back against the blocks.
  4. Check whether geogrid reinforcement is specified: Taller walls or walls supporting driveways or steep upper slopes typically need horizontal geogrid layers anchored deep into compacted backfill.
A retaining wall that begins leaning outward, bulging mid-height, or opening wide cracks behind the upper soil slope signals active ground movement—keep heavy vehicles back from the crest and have a qualified engineer assess stability.
